Definitions

Current Inventory Status
Movement rate

The share of inventory classified as Active vs. Slow Moving, based on recent usage history.

Slotting priority

A score indicating how urgently an item should be placed for easy access, based on pick frequency — shown here crossed with each item's PO risk level.

Storage type

The physical storage class assigned to an item — Pallets, Drawers, Boneyard, and others — based on handling needs.

Supply continuity risk

Whether current on-hand inventory is likely to cover demand until the next delivery, or is at risk of stockout first.

Procurement exposure

The dollar value of open purchase commitments tied to an item — a proxy for financial risk concentration.

How to Read This Tab

Current Inventory Status
  1. 1The gauge shows the overall share of inventory that's active (green) vs. slow-moving (red), across all tracked items.
  2. 2Search any item code, description, or priority level in the search box to filter the priority × PO risk breakdown, the storage-by-priority bars, and the item list all at once.
  3. 3The priority × PO risk breakdown shades each cell by how many items fall into it — darker means more items — with row and column totals on the edges.
  4. 4Switch between the High/Medium/Low tabs on the storage breakdown to see where each tier's inventory physically lives.
  5. 5Service availability shows the share of items where on-hand stock may not last until the next delivery, across all tracked items.
  6. 6Use the dropdowns on the item list for a second layer of filtering by priority, storage type, or PO risk level. The colored strip on each card reflects PO risk (red/yellow/green).
  7. 7Click a bar on the exposure leaderboard to filter every visual above down to just that item; click it again (or clear the search box) to reset.

Definitions

Min-Max Optimization
NIVR (Net Inventory Value Reduction)

Estimated total capital freed up when shifting from current policy to the selected min-max policy.

Lead Time Variability

Simulated vendor supply delay parameters (Normal, 15% Delay, 30% Delay) used to stress-test safety stock thresholds.

Demand Profile

A simulated demand pattern (Stable, Moderate, Spiky) representing how predictable or volatile item usage is expected to be.

Policy Type

The replenishment strategy being modeled — Balanced, High Service, or Lean — each trading off inventory cost against service level.

Min-Max Recommendation

The suggested minimum and maximum stock thresholds for an item under the selected scenario, compared against current thresholds.

How to Read This Tab

Min-Max Optimization
  1. 1The KPI cards summarize the optimization model as a whole: how many parts were evaluated, the total capital that could be freed up (NIVR), and how many recommendations were validated.
  2. 2Use the Lead Time, Demand Profile, and Policy Type toggles below to pick a scenario — the Replenishment Workload multiple, the policy comparison, and the exposure leaderboard all update to match.
  3. 3The Current vs. Selected Policy card compares today's inventory value and fill rate against what the chosen scenario would produce.
  4. 4The Inventory Value Reduction leaderboard ranks the items driving the largest savings under the selected Policy Type — this ranking depends only on Policy Type, not Lead Time or Demand.
